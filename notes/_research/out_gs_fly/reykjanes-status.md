# reykjanes-status

**Q:** As of September 2026, what is the status of the Reykjanes peninsula in Iceland: is there an active volcanic eruption, is Grindavík accessible, is the Blue Lagoon open, and are Gunnuhver, Bridge Between Continents, Brimketill, Valahnúkamöl, Reykjanesviti lighthouse, and Krýsuvík/Seltún open to visitors? Any road closures on routes 425 and 42?

As of early September 2026, the Reykjanes Peninsula remains a geologically dynamic area. While there is no active volcanic eruption, the region is in a state of heightened alert, and access to certain areas is subject to change based on seismic activity and official assessments.

### **Volcanic Activity**

As of September 2026, there is no ongoing volcanic eruption on the Reykjanes Peninsula. The most recent eruption concluded on August 5, 2025. However, magma accumulation continues beneath the Svartsengi area, and the Icelandic Meteorological Office considers a new eruption a likely scenario in the near future. This means that the situation can change with little warning, and visitors should always consult official sources like the Icelandic Met Office (vedur.is) and SafeTravel.is before heading to the peninsula.

### **Accessibility of Grindavík**

Access to the town of Grindavík is variable. While the town is not entirely closed off, access is managed by authorities and can be restricted based on safety conditions. Potential hazards include ground fissures, gas pollution, and sudden earthquakes. Full-time residency for many inhabitants has not yet been permitted, and the area is not considered a traditional tourist destination at this time due to the ongoing geological instability.

### **Status of Tourist Sites**

*   **Blue Lagoon:** The Blue Lagoon is open for visitors, but its operating status can fluctuate depending on volcanic activity and safety assessments. It has experienced temporary closures since late 2023 due to its proximity to the recent eruption zones. It is essential to check the Blue Lagoon's official website for the most current information and to book tickets in advance.

*   **Gunnuhver, Bridge Between Continents, Brimketill, Valahnúkamöl, and Reykjanesviti Lighthouse:** These sites on the western part of the peninsula are generally accessible.
    *   **Gunnuhver:** The geothermal area with its large mud pool is open, with boardwalks and viewing platforms for safe viewing.
    *   **Bridge Between Continents:** This footbridge over the tectonic plate rift is open to visitors and is a popular, quick stop.
    *   **Brimketill:** The natural rock pool can be viewed from a designated platform. Swimming is not permitted due to dangerous waves.
    *   **Valahnúkamöl:** This dramatic, rocky coastline is accessible, offering views of the Atlantic.
    *   **Reykjanesviti:** Iceland's oldest lighthouse can be visited, although the structure itself is not open to the public.

*   **Krýsuvík/Seltún:** The geothermal area of Krýsuvík, including the colorful Seltún fields, is open to visitors. There are marked paths and boardwalks to ensure safety around the hot springs and mud pots.

### **Road Closures**

The status of roads on the peninsula can change quickly.

*   **Route 43 (Grindavíkurvegur):** This main road to Grindavík and the Blue Lagoon has been repeatedly closed and rebuilt due to lava flows. Its accessibility is not guaranteed.

*   **Route 425 (Nesvegur):** This road runs along the western tip of the peninsula, providing access to Gunnuhver, the Bridge Between Continents, and Reykjanesviti. Parts of this road may close, often in conjunction with closures of Route 43, especially the sections closer to Grindavík.

*   **Route 42 (Krýsuvíkurvegur):** This road leads to the Krýsuvík/Seltún geothermal area. It is generally open but is a lower priority for maintenance, which can affect access, particularly in winter conditions.

Travelers are strongly advised to check the latest road conditions on the official Icelandic Road and Coastal Administration website (road.is) before any travel to the Reykjanes Peninsula.
