#### **2. One-Way Fee (Einveisleie) and Pricing**

*   **One-Way Fee:** A one-way fee, or "einveisleie," is almost always charged for dropping a car at a different location. This fee is not standardized and varies significantly based on the company, distance, and demand. While a precise figure for EVE to Svolvær is only available during a direct booking, expect it to range from **500 NOK to over 1,500 NOK** ($50 - $150+ USD). Some forum users have reported fees as high as $500 for longer one-way routes in Norway, so it's a significant cost to verify. No major companies appear to outright refuse the EVE to Svolvær route, but availability can be limited, so booking well in advance is crucial.

*   **Typical 3-Day Price (Compact/Mid-SUV):** Car rental prices in Lofoten are highly seasonal. For early October, which is the shoulder season, you can expect the following daily rates for a compact or mid-size SUV:
    *   **Approximately 1,000 - 2,000 NOK per day.**
    *   This translates to a total of **3,000 - 6,000 NOK** for a 3-day rental, excluding the one-way fee and other extras.
    *   Booking comparison sites show average SUV rental costs in Lofoten around $215 per day (approx. 2,200 NOK), while smaller cars are cheaper.

*   **Winter Tyres:** The law in Norway requires winter tyres (or tyres with sufficient grip) when road conditions are wintry. For Northern Norway (Nordland, Troms, and Finnmark), the period where studded tyres are permitted begins on October 16th. For your rental dates (Sept 30 - Oct 2), you are on the cusp of the winter season. Rental companies are responsible for ensuring cars have appropriate tyres for the conditions. It is highly probable that cars in Lofoten will be fitted with non-studded winter tyres (`piggfrie dekk`) by this time, as temperatures can easily drop below freezing. You should confirm this with the rental agency when booking.

#### **3. Driving Routes, Times, and Tolls**

The main road through Lofoten is the **E10**, which is a designated National Tourist Route. The roads are generally well-maintained but can be narrow.

*   **Route EVE to Svolvær:**
    *   **Distance:** Approximately 165 km.
    *   **Driving Time:** Around 2 hours and 30 minutes without stops.
    *   **Route:** Follow the E10 highway west from the airport area. The route is straightforward and takes you directly to Svolvær.

*   **Route EVE to Reine:**
    *   **Distance:** Approximately 290 km.
    *   **Driving Time:** Around 4 hours and 15 minutes without stops.
    *   **Route:** Follow the E10 from the airport, passing Svolvær, Leknes, and continuing towards the southern end of the archipelago until you reach Reine.

*   **Tolls, Ferries, and Tunnels:**
    *   **AutoPASS System:** Norway uses an automated toll system called AutoPASS. All rental cars are equipped with an AutoPASS transponder tag in the windshield. You do not need to stop or do anything when passing a toll station; the charge is registered automatically.
    *   **Tolls on Your Route:** The main E10 route from Evenes through the Lofoten Islands is largely **free of road tolls**. The major toll projects are typically around the large cities, not on this specific scenic route. There are no ferries required to drive from EVE to Svolvær or Reine.
    *   **Billing:** The rental company pays the toll and will then charge the accumulated amount to your credit card after the rental period, often with a small administrative fee (check the rental agreement).

#### **4. Norwegian Traffic Rules for Foreign Drivers**

Driving in Norway is safe, but rules are strictly enforced with high fines.

*   **International Driving Permit (IDP):**
    *   **For a Chinese Driver's License: YES, an IDP is required.** The Norwegian Public Roads Administration (Statens vegvesen) and various sources confirm that if your license is not printed in the Latin alphabet, you must carry either an International Driving Permit (IDP) or an official translation. Rental agencies will likely refuse the rental without an IDP.
    *   **For a US Driver's License:** An IDP is not legally required as the license is in English, but it is highly recommended.

*   **Speed Limits:** Limits are strictly enforced by numerous fixed cameras, mobile patrols, and average-speed cameras (`streknings-ATK`).
    *   **Residential areas:** 30 km/h.
    *   **Built-up/Urban areas:** 50 km/h.
    *   **Rural roads:** 80 km/h (this is the default on most of the E10).
    *   **Motorways/Dual Carriageways:** 90-110 km/h (rare in Lofoten).
    *   **Fines are severe.** Even exceeding the limit by a small amount (e.g., 10 km/h) can result in a fine of over 2,500 NOK.

*   **Mandatory Headlights:** Dipped headlights must be on **at all times**, 24 hours a day, regardless of weather or time of day. Most modern rental cars manage this automatically.

*   **Animals on the Road:** Be extremely cautious of sheep and reindeer, especially in unfenced areas. They can appear on the road suddenly. Slow down immediately and wait for them to pass. Do not honk, as it may startle them.

*   **Single-Lane Roads & Passing Places:** You will encounter narrow roads, including some single-lane sections with designated passing places (`møteplass`).
    *   The rule is to yield to oncoming traffic if the passing place is on your side of the road.
    *   The driver heading downhill should generally yield to the one heading uphill.
    *   Do not park in these passing places; they are strictly for allowing traffic to pass.

*   **Other Key Rules:**
    *   **Give Way:** You must give way to traffic from the right at intersections unless you are on a priority road (marked with a yellow diamond sign).
    *   **Pedestrians:** Always stop for pedestrians at marked zebra crossings.
    *   **Alcohol Limit:** Norway has a very strict blood alcohol limit of 0.02%. The rule is effectively zero tolerance; do not drink any alcohol before driving.
