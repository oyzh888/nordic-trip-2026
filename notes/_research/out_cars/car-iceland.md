### 1. Vehicle Choice: 4x4/AWD vs. 2WD and Winter Tires

For your late September trip covering paved roads along the south coast, Golden Circle, and Snaefellsnes peninsula, a **2WD vehicle is generally sufficient**. The main roads on your itinerary are part of the Ring Road, which is well-maintained. However, Icelandic weather in September is unpredictable and can include strong winds, heavy rain, and even early snowfall. For this reason, a **4x4/AWD vehicle such as a Dacia Duster or Toyota RAV4 is highly recommended** for increased stability, safety, and comfort, especially on gravel sections or in case of unexpected weather changes.

Regarding tires, the law in Iceland mandates the use of winter tires from **November 1st to April 15th**. Therefore, for your rental period at the end of September, winter tires are **not legally required**. However, many rental companies start equipping their fleets with winter tires as a precaution towards the end of the month.

### 3. Understanding Car Rental Insurance in Iceland

Navigating car rental insurance in Iceland can be complex, but understanding the acronyms is the first step to ensuring you are adequately covered.

*   **CDW (Collision Damage Waiver):** This is a basic insurance that is almost always included in the rental price. It limits your financial liability to a certain deductible (self-risk amount) in case of a collision. This deductible can be quite high, often ranging from €1,500 to €3,500.
*   **SCDW (Super Collision Damage Waiver):** This is an optional upgrade that significantly reduces the deductible of the standard CDW, sometimes to zero.
*   **GP (Gravel Protection):** This is a crucial insurance for Iceland as many roads, even sections of the main Ring Road, can be unpaved. It covers damage to the windshield, headlights, and car body from stones thrown up by other vehicles. Standard CDW/SCDW does not typically cover this.
*   **SAAP (Sand and Ash Protection):** This protects against damage from sand and volcanic ash storms, which can strip the paint, scratch windows, and damage the engine. This is highly recommended, especially for travel along the South Coast.
*   **TP (Theft Protection):** This covers the theft of the rental vehicle. Given Iceland's low crime rate, this is often seen as less critical than other protections but is usually bundled with other insurance packages.
*   **Windscreen Cover:** This specifically covers damage to the front windscreen, which is a common issue due to gravel. It is often included in Gravel Protection but can sometimes be a separate add-on.

**Must-Buy Insurance:** For a worry-free trip, it is highly recommended to purchase a comprehensive insurance package from the rental company that includes **SCDW, GP, and SAAP**. These cover the most common and costly damages specific to Icelandic conditions.

**Credit Card CDW:** Relying solely on credit card rental insurance is **not recommended** in Iceland. Many credit card policies have significant exclusions for the types of vehicles common in Iceland (like 4x4s and campervans) and for damages unique to the country, such as those from gravel, sand, and wind. If you do choose to use your credit card's insurance, you will likely have to pay for any damages upfront and then seek reimbursement from your card provider, a process that can be lengthy and is not guaranteed to cover all costs.

For a 4-day rental of a mid-size AWD SUV like a **Dacia Duster** or **Toyota RAV4** from September 26th to 29th, 2026, including full insurance (SCDW, GP, SAAP, and often a zero-deductible option) and a second driver, you can expect a total price in the range of **€450 to €700 (approximately 67,000 to 104,000 ISK)**.

This estimate is based on typical pricing from recommended local companies and includes:
*   **Base rental cost:** This varies by company and how far in advance you book.
*   **Full insurance package:** This can add approximately €20-€40 per day.
*   **Second driver fee:** This typically costs around €5-€15 per day.
*   **New Road Tax (as of 2026):** A daily fee of around 1,400 to 1,550 ISK (€9-€11) is now charged by rental companies.

Here are some example price ranges for a Dacia Duster per day, which can fluctuate based on demand and booking time:

*   **Green Light Car Rental:** From around 9,800 ISK per day.
*   **Reykjavik Cars:** A 7-day rental can be around $1,275 ($182 per day).

To get an exact price, it is best to get a quote directly from the rental companies' websites for your specific dates.

### 5. Pickup Location: Reykjavik City vs. Keflavik Airport (KEF)

For your itinerary, picking up your rental car at **Keflavik Airport (KEF) is generally the more convenient and often more cost-effective option**.

*   **Cost:** While some destinations have significant airport surcharges, this is not typically the case in Iceland. In fact, renting from a city location might be slightly more expensive. The main cost difference to consider is the airport transfer. A bus or taxi from KEF to Reykjavik costs a minimum of €30 per person. If you have multiple people in your group, this can quickly exceed any potential savings from a city rental.
*   **Convenience:** Picking up at KEF allows you to start your journey immediately upon arrival. Your itinerary includes destinations like the Blue Lagoon, which is very close to the airport, making an airport pickup highly practical.
*   **One-Way Fees:** If you were to pick up in Reykjavik and drop off at KEF, most companies will charge a one-way fee, typically around €50.
